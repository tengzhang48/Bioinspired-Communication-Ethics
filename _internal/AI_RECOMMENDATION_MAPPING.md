# AI Ethics Recommendation Mapping

## Draft Summary

The current Bioinspired Communication and Ethics course already includes substantial AI-related content across the syllabus and multiple modules. Rather than treating AI ethics as a standalone add-on, the course currently uses a distributed model: AI appears in proposal writing, scientific writing, and research ethics, with explicit attention to disclosure, hallucinations, peer review, authorship, and responsible use. This means the reviewers' recommendation is best understood not as building AI ethics from scratch, but as formalizing, deepening, and assessing work that is already underway.

At the course level, the syllabus already names AI use as a learning objective and includes an explicit AI use policy requiring disclosure and prohibiting AI-generated citations and references in student work. See [syllabus.md](syllabus.md#L51), [syllabus.md](syllabus.md#L54), and [syllabus.md](syllabus.md#L153). AI is then developed in three places: proposal writing in [modules/proposal.md](modules/proposal.md#L311), scientific writing and AI-feedback comparison in [modules/paper.md](modules/paper.md#L305), and research ethics, authorship, and AI governance in [modules/ethics.md](modules/ethics.md#L249).

## What Has Been Done

The course already has a strong foundation for AI ethics. AI is not treated as an afterthought; it is threaded through the curriculum as a research practice, writing practice, and ethics problem. Current materials already cover disclosure, hallucinations, reliability limits, AI-assisted peer review, authorship, ghostwriting, bias, regulatory questions, and responsible communication. The course also already uses the pedagogical methods the reviewers recommend: team-based learning, case studies, peer review, mock panels, and reflective discussion.

In practice, the strongest existing pieces are:
- A syllabus-level AI use policy in [syllabus.md](syllabus.md#L153)
- AI in proposal development and critique in [modules/proposal.md](modules/proposal.md#L311)
- AI versus human feedback in scientific writing in [modules/paper.md](modules/paper.md#L305)
- AI ethics, authorship, disclosure, and governance in [modules/ethics.md](modules/ethics.md#L249)

## What Is Missing

What is missing is not basic AI ethics coverage, but formalization. The course would benefit from clearer AI-specific learning outcomes, a consistent disclosure/reporting framework, assignment prompts that require students to document AI use, and rubrics that evaluate ethical AI practice directly. The current materials also do not show program-level implementation pieces such as a microcredential, mentor training, seminar integration, or NRT assessment updates, because those items are outside the scope of this repository.

The main gaps are:
- No explicit cross-course AI ethics framework tying the three AI-related units together
- No standard disclosure statement for proposals, manuscripts, presentations, and team reports
- No rubric criteria for ethical AI use, transparent reporting, or peer review of AI-assisted work
- No explicit assessment language for AI literacy as a competency
- No evidence in this repository of program-level mentor training, badges, or evaluation-plan revisions

## Bottom Line

A fair summary is that the recommendation is directionally right, but it understates the work already completed. The course already incorporates significant AI ethics content. The next step is not to invent AI ethics coverage from scratch, but to consolidate existing material into a more explicit, assessable, and reportable structure that aligns with the reviewers' language.
