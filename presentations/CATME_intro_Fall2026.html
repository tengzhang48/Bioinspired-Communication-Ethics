Team-forming survey · September 9

The one mistake almost everyone makes

The schedule grid asks when you are busy and unavailable. It is not a when-are-you-free poll. Check the blocks you cannot meet.

Everyone should mark our class block as busy: Monday and Wednesday, [class time]. Also mark standing commitments: lab, TA duties, seminars, care responsibilities, standing meetings.

The survey also asks your program, year, programming and writing experience, hours you plan to spend outside class, and how you prefer to lead or follow. Answer honestly. The algorithm can only build workable teams from accurate input, and schedule compatibility is weighted most heavily.

The instrument

Five dimensions

  • C
    Contributing to the Team's WorkDoes the share of work, on time, at usable quality.
  • I
    Interacting with TeammatesCommunicates, listens, keeps everyone informed, handles conflict.
  • K
    Keeping the Team on TrackTracks progress, notices when things slip, gives teammates feedback.
  • E
    Expecting QualityHolds the team to a high standard and says so out loud.
  • H
    Having Relevant Knowledge, Skills, and AbilitiesBrings the ability the task needs, and picks up what's missing.

Before you rate anyone

Three is the expectation, not a disappointment

The middle description is what a perfectly functioning team looks like. Save the top description for someone who genuinely stood out. Use the bottom one when expectations were genuinely not met.

Inflating everyone to 5 makes the whole instrument useless. It also makes it impossible to tell your team anything they can act on. A 3 says the work got done well; that is a good outcome.

Rate in private. Don't compare answers with your team beforehand.

Two distinctions worth remembering

Where people disagree

Doing the work is not the same as having the ability.
"Completes the math required" is contributing. "Figures out how to do the math" is knowledge and skill. One is effort, the other is capability.

Giving feedback is keeping the team on track, not interacting.
Interacting covers communication and listening. Noticing that something needs to change, and saying so, is track-keeping.

Peer-to-peer comments

Write something they can act on

Address the person directly. "They were often late to meetings." → "Arriving with your section drafted would give us more time to revise."

Respectful and actionable. Name a behavior and a change, not a trait. "Disorganized" is not usable; "post the draft the night before so we can read it" is.

On a team of three, assume your comments are attributable. Two teammates, two sets of comments. Write only what you would say to their face, because functionally you are.

There is a separate box for confidential comments to me. Use it for anything you don't want your team to see.

Grading

Three rounds of feedback, one that counts

Evaluations 1, 2, and 3 are formative. Only the fourth affects your grade.

That is deliberate. Early low ratings are useful information, not a penalty, and you have the rest of the semester to respond to them. Someone who takes the feedback and changes should not be carrying the first-round score in December.

Completing each evaluation is required, including the ones that don't affect your grade. I can't return results to your team until everyone has submitted.
