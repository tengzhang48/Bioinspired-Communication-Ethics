# Reviewer Meeting Talking Points: AI Ethics Integration

## Core Message

The course already includes meaningful AI ethics content. The main issue is not absence, but presentation and formalization. For the meeting, the most useful position is: we already have a strong foundation, and we can respond to the reviewers by making that existing work more explicit, more assessable, and easier to communicate.

## Opening Framing

- We appreciate the reviewers' emphasis on AI ethics, transparency, and responsible research practice.
- Their comments are directionally useful.
- At the same time, the current course already addresses AI ethics in substantial ways across multiple modules.
- Our next step is not to build AI ethics from scratch, but to organize and strengthen what is already there.

## What Is Already in Place

- The syllabus already includes AI-related learning goals and an AI use policy.
- Students are already asked to disclose AI use and are explicitly warned against using AI-generated citations or references.
- The proposal module already addresses AI in research, including hypothesis generation, AI-assisted review, and limits of AI judgment.
- The scientific writing module already compares AI feedback with human peer review and asks students to evaluate hallucinations and attribution failures.
- The research ethics module already covers AI ghostwriting, bias, detection failure, authorship, accountability, and governance.
- The pedagogy is already aligned with the reviewers' recommendations: team-based learning, peer review, debates, mock panels, and case-based discussion.

## Distribution Adjustment We Could Make

- It would be useful to introduce AI earlier in Module 1 as part of team formation and shared expectations.
- That early discussion could cover responsible use of AI in collaborative work, disclosure norms, and the effect of AI tools on communication and accountability.
- This would make AI part of the course's teamwork foundation, not just a later ethics topic.

## What We Can Honestly Say to Reviewers

- AI ethics is already integrated across the course rather than confined to a single isolated lecture.
- The course already treats AI as a research ethics issue, a writing issue, a peer review issue, and a professional responsibility issue.
- We agree that this strand can be made more visible and more systematic.

## What Is Missing

- The AI content is strong, but the structure is implicit rather than explicitly labeled as a course-wide AI ethics strand.
- We do not yet have a standard AI disclosure template for student proposals, manuscripts, presentations, or reports.
- We do not yet have rubric language that explicitly evaluates ethical AI use and transparent reporting.
- Some program-level recommendations, such as mentor training, seminar programming, or microcredentials, are outside the scope of this course repository and should be discussed at the NRT program level.

## A Practical Near-Term Step

- A useful immediate response would be to invite the reviewer to give a guest lecture, potentially by Zoom.
- The best fit would be the dedicated AI session in Module 4: "Artificial Intelligence in Research."
- That session already covers AI ghostwriting, hallucinations, detection, and regulatory frameworks, so the match is strong.
- This would let students benefit directly from the reviewer's expertise while keeping the overall course structure intact.

## Best Strategic Response

- Acknowledge the reviewers' recommendations positively.
- Emphasize that the course already contains a strong base in AI ethics.
- Position our response as refinement and formalization rather than a wholesale redesign.
- Commit to clearer learning outcomes, structured disclosure guidance, and stronger assessment language.

## On the Synthetic Biology Section

- The synthetic biology discussion is interesting, but too narrow to organize the entire course response.
- AI ethics and synthetic biology ethics are each large fields with their own debates.
- We should avoid making "AI in synthetic biology" the umbrella for the course.
- A better approach is to use synthetic biology as one optional case-study area within a broader AI ethics framework.

## Recommended Organizing Framework Going Forward

- Transparent AI use and disclosure.
- Reliability, hallucinations, bias, and limits of AI judgment.
- Authorship, peer review, accountability, and intellectual responsibility.
- Domain-specific case studies, with synthetic biology as one example rather than the defining frame.

## Suggested Verbal Close

"Our course already contains substantial AI ethics content across proposal writing, scientific writing, and research ethics. We see the reviewers' comments as an opportunity to make that existing work more explicit and more assessable, rather than as a call to start from scratch. We also think it is important to keep the framework broad: synthetic biology can be one useful case study, but it should not define the full scope of AI ethics in the course."

"A practical next step could be to introduce AI expectations earlier in the team-building module and invite the reviewer to give a Zoom guest lecture for the dedicated AI session in Module 4, where topics like ghostwriting, hallucinations, detection, and regulation are already a natural fit."
